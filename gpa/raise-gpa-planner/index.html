How the planner math works

Open the free GPA tool and fill past credits and past CGPA. Type your goal, like 3.5. Type next credits, like 15. The tool blends old and new work. It finds the next average that lifts the full mix to your goal.

Say you hold 30 credits at 2.8 and you take 15 more. Your past gives 84 points. To end at 3.0 for 45 credits, you need 135 points. That leaves 51 points for 15 credits. That is a 3.4 average next term. That math is firm. Wishing will not bend it.

If the goal sits past the top of the scale, the tool warns you. A 4.5 ask on a 4.0 scale cannot land. That warn is a gift. It tells you to shift the goal, add more credits, or stretch the plan past one term.

Retake honesty: what counts and what does not

Retakes help most when your school drops the old grade. The tool has a replace switch for that case. With it on, only the best try in each course counts. With it off, each try counts.

Here is the catch. Many schools do not drop the old grade. Some take the mean. Some cap how many times you can try. Some cap how high the new grade can count. So treat the tool view as one case, not a vow. Read your school rules. Then ask to be sure. For scale basics first, see our how GPA works guide.

Talk to an advisor with facts in hand

Bring past credits, past CGPA, goal, and next credits. Bring the list of courses you plan to take. Ask how retakes count at your school. Ask what load fits your week. Ask what pass cut your course uses.

Small steps beat big leaps. A 3.9 ask in one term may break you. A 3.3 ask twice in a row may land the same CGPA with less strain. Pick the path you can keep up all term. Your grades stay on your device while you plan.

Raise GPA questions

How does the GPA target planner do its math?

It blends past credits and past CGPA with next credits and next grades. It finds the next average that lifts the full mix to your goal. If the goal sits past the top of the scale, it warns you it cannot be met.

Do retakes erase my old grade?

Not always. Some schools take the best try, some take the mean of both tries, and some cap retakes. The tool has a replace switch to show the best try case. Treat it as a guess and check your school rules.

What should I ask a school advisor?

Ask how retakes count, how many credits you can take next term, and what pass cut your course uses. Bring your past credits, past CGPA, and your goal so they can check the math with you.
